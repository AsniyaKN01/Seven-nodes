Disease Biology Insights: T-ALL

Multi-omics analysis of Acute T-Cell Lymphoblastic Leukemia — synthesised from peer-reviewed PubMed publications and curated genomic databases.
264 cases analysed 106 driver genes 10 recurrent pathways Updated Feb 2026
Summary

T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (T-ALL) is an aggressive haematological malignancy of immature T-cell progenitors, comprising 10–15% of paediatric and approximately 25% of adult ALL cases. Integrated genomic analysis of large cohorts has identified NOTCH1 activating mutations as the most prevalent driver event, present in over 50% of cases, with co-occurring FBXW7 loss disrupting NOTCH1 protein degradation.

The tumour suppressor CDKN2A is deleted in approximately 70% of T-ALL, promoting unchecked cell cycle progression. Deregulated expression of T-cell transcription factors — including TAL1, LMO1/LMO2, TLX1, and TLX3 — drives stage-specific differentiation arrest. Recent single-cell multiomic studies have identified a bone marrow progenitor-like (BMP-like) leukemia subpopulation associated with treatment resistance and poor survival, with therapeutic vulnerability to the BCL-2 inhibitor venetoclax.

Disruption of oxidative phosphorylation (OxPhos) using mitochondrial complex I inhibitors, combined with L-asparaginase to exploit glutamine dependency, has shown profound tumour reduction in pre-clinical T-ALL models. The JAK-STAT, PI3K/AKT, and RAS-MAPK signalling pathways represent additional actionable therapeutic axes, with pathway-subtype associations guiding stratified treatment approaches.

Key Insights
1

NOTCH1 is the dominant oncogenic driver

Activating NOTCH1 mutations occur in >50% of T-ALL cases, affecting thymocyte specification and development. NOTCH1 mutations additively drive blasts away from treatment-resistant BMP-like states, suggesting a paradoxical role in chemosensitivity.

2

Treatment-resistant BMP-like subpopulation identified

Single-cell multiomics revealed a bone marrow progenitor-like leukemia cell population associated with treatment failure and poor overall survival. Its molecular signature predicts poor outcome across multiple T-ALL subtypes.

3

Venetoclax shows vulnerability in resistant cells

In silico and in vitro drug screening identified apoptosis-inducing agents, particularly the BCL-2 inhibitor venetoclax, as therapeutically effective against BMP-like blast populations that resist conventional chemotherapy.

4

OxPhos + L-asparaginase creates synthetic lethality

Inhibition of mitochondrial complex I (IACS-010759) forces metabolic reprogramming into glutaminolysis. Combined with L-asparaginase, this creates a synthetic lethal interaction with profound tumour reduction in NOTCH1-mutated T-ALL.

5

Pathway-subtype associations guide stratification

NRAS/FLT3 mutations associate with immature T-ALL; JAK3/STAT5B with HOXA-deregulated cases; PTPN2 with TLX1-driven disease; and PIK3R1/PTEN with TAL1-deregulated ALL — enabling precision therapeutic targeting.
